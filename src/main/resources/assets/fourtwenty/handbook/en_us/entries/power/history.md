Nobody invented Boundless Inspiration. It was noticed, by someone lying down, which is how a surprising amount of Weedcraft's history has been made.

# The Sandwich
The first recorded case belongs to **Willard**, who sat down beside a Terpinator that had refused to start all morning, lit a joint, unwrapped a sandwich, and watched the machine start up by itself. Willard did not get up to investigate. Willard finished the sandwich. This set the tone for the whole field.

# Kerberos and the Push
**Kerberos** was the first to suspect that the machine wasn't running on Willard so much as on something coming *off* Willard, and spent a year trying to catch it: first in a jar, then in a bigger jar, then, briefly and regrettably, in a barn. Kerberos was famously persuasive, and once talked a creeper into turning round and walking away. There were no other witnesses, but Kerberos tells it very well.

The barn never worked either. But Kerberos noticed that the something *pushed*: harder from a big smoke than a small one, and harder still from a Hookah. Push is voltage, and the unit of push is the **Kerberos**.

# Ignus and the Flow
**Ignus** realised that push was only half of it. Something had to actually *go* somewhere, and how much of it went was a different question from how hard it was pushed. Ignus made this discovery by holding both ends of a live Wick at once, an experiment this book does not recommend, Ignus does not remember, and Ignus's hair has never entirely recovered from. Flow is current, and the unit of flow is the **Ignus**.

(In real electricity, it's the flow through you that does the damage, not the push. Remember that one.)

# Willard, Again
With push and flow pinned down, somebody had to multiply them, and the job went to Willard, who had not moved since the sandwich. Push times flow is **power**: not how much work gets done, but how *fast*. Willard, who did a great deal of work in short bursts and then lay down for the rest of the day, took this as a personal tribute. The unit of power is the **Willard**.

# Phu Objects
**Phu** objected. Phu objected to the jar, to the barn, to the Wick, to the multiplying, and at some length to the whole idea of naming units after people. The committee, which was mostly Willard, decided that anybody who could slow down that many good ideas had earned the unit of resistance. Phu objected to that too.

Resistance is measured in **Phus** (one Phu, two Phus), and its symbol is the Greek letter Φ, which Phu also objected to, on the grounds that nobody can agree how to say it.

# Garcia and Marley
The **Garcia** (capacitance) and the **Marley** (inductance) came much later, named by a second committee after whatever was playing at the time. That committee kept no minutes, and nobody who was there can remember which song.

# So What Is It?
Nobody knows for sure. Kerberos's theory is that inspiration behaves like water: it runs downhill, and a Weedcrafter who's high is standing at the top of the hill. Phu's theory is that Kerberos is wrong. Either way, BI obeys exactly the same laws as electricity, down to the last decimal place, which is why [Inspoweednetics](entry:electronics/welcome) can teach you both at once.
