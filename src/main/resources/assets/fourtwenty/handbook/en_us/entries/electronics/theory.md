Four ideas explain almost all of electronics. Think of water in a pipe.

# Voltage: the Push
**Voltage** is how hard the power pushes, like water pressure. The mod measures it in **Kerberos (Kb)**; the real unit is the **volt**. A Hookah's Puff power pushes at 4.2 Kb.

# Current: the Flow
**Current** is how much power flows past, like litres a second. The mod's unit is the **Ignus (Ig)**; the real one is the **ampere**.

# Resistance: the Squeeze
**Resistance** is how hard something makes it for the flow to pass, like a narrow pipe. The mod's unit is the **Phus (Φ)**; the real one is the **ohm**. The part that adds resistance is the **Resinator**.

# Power: the Work Done
**Power** is voltage times current: push times flow. The mod's unit is the **Willard (W)**; the real one is the **watt**. Over time, power adds up as energy, in **Willard-seconds (Ws)**: a real **joule**.

# Ohm's Law
Push, flow and squeeze are tied together by one rule: **voltage = current × resistance**. Double the squeeze and the flow halves. Double the push and the flow doubles. Everything else is built on it.

# In a Row and Side by Side
Two parts wired **one after another** are **in series**: the same current goes through both, and they share the push. A **Daisy Wick** does this. Two parts wired **side by side** are **in parallel**: each gets the full push, and they share the flow. A **Twin Wick** does this.

# Seeing It for Yourself
Hold an **Inspoweedometer** and look at any Wick or part: it reads the volts, amps, watts and ohms live on your action bar. Use it on a block to hold that reading while you check another. It never changes anything it reads, so measure freely.
