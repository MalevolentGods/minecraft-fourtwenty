The loop

Each machine has one job. You discover at the Infuser and produce at the Terpinator.

Chase itInspiration Infuser: drop resin into the pool until it matches.

Why you'll want to chase it

The vision

  • When you smoke, the colour floods your screen in Minecraft: a soft full-screen wash of the inspired colour that blooms and fades, with a whispered line ("…something like this").
  • It doesn't fully leave. Until you've matched it, a faint wash of that colour lingers at the screen's edges when you're near the Infuser, an itch you'll want to scratch.
  • Each hit inspires you with one or two of the strain's colours, so visions pile up, and more hits make each one truer. Inspiration never fades.
  • Everyone starts fully inspired by Green, the colour of the plant, so the first visit to the Infuser has the whole experience: solid colour and green/red feedback.

The payoff

  • The bead that catches it settles the pool on the exact colour, so it melts into the ring around it, and a rainbow swirls up around both. It names the colour and its terpene and adds it to your collection. The pool is then sealed: no more beads until you drain it.
  • Unmatched visions sit in the collection as glowing blanks with no name: you've seen them, but you haven't caught them yet.
  • Every match unlocks blocks to build with (hemp, concrete, lamps, rugs), and every new terpene can become a new strain, which means new visions.

The pool

Drops and the pool

  • A pool of liquid weed magic, clear and shimmering. Each tap drops a bead of resin in: it falls, ripples and swirls through.
  • Four bead sizes: glob, drop, drip, speck. A glob is 32 drops; a speck is half a drip. The recipe card counts them.
  • The pool holds 12 globs of resin. Past that it won't take more.
  • It's all about ratios. The pool's colour depends on the proportions of resin, not the amount: 1 Moonmilk to 1 Void Tar is the same grey at two specks or two globs.
  • Swell ×2 doubles everything in the pool. The colour doesn't change, but every bead after it is a finer adjustment. Close but overshooting? Swell, then fine-tune.
  • Take it back undoes the last three moves; Drain the pool starts over. It's all free: experimenting never costs anything.

Reading the pool

  • The inspired colour rings the pool and touches its edge exactly, so you compare them side by side, pixel against pixel.
  • At low inspiration the ring breathes in and out on a slow cycle, staying on a little longer with each level, until at full inspiration it stays. Then its outer edge answers one question after every bead: can this pool still become a proper recipe for this colour? Green means yes, whatever order you go in. A proper recipe is one of the colour's real routes (1 to 3 resins that make it on their own), with a little wiggle room (up to 4% of anything else). Red means no: something's in there that a proper recipe can't absorb in the space left, so take it back. It never says no to a real route, and it doesn't reward drowning a mistake.
  • The map is where you choose. A full colour map, washed in green. Every colour you're inspired by shows through the green in its true colour, faintly at first and truer with each hit. Once you're fully inspired by a colour, its exact spot is marked with a swatch and crosshairs. You can be inspired by many colours at once: tap one to chase it. Your pool is the little ring on the map.
  • The Δ (how far off you are) is always shown, in Japanese numerals. Match at Δ 1.0 or less; Spot On ★ at 0.5 or less. Most people stop seeing a difference around 1.

The resins

Twelve resins, built as a warm and a cool version of each primary. The wrong-leaning blue makes a muddy purple, opposites cancel to grey, and the dark ones take over fast. Under the hood the pool follows real pigment physics (light absorbed and scattered across the spectrum), so it behaves the way your eye expects colour to mix.

Difficulty and the recipe audit

All 80 colours (the 16 Minecraft colours and the 64 hemp colours) have a target that is a real recipe: a few resins in small whole-number parts, using the resins you have at that stage (the 8 starters, plus the family's own resin for hemp colours). Recipes are chosen for colour accuracy and simplicity, and each one has to pass a test player who starts with a glob of one of the recipe's resins and then only ever takes the single bead that the green/red feedback says helps most (plus Swell). All 80 pass. The Minecraft colours all take 10 moves or fewer; the median across all 80 is 5 or 6, and the hardest takes 14.

White

White chased Minecraft's exact white, which needed a full pool plus specks of strong blue. Now white is just Moonmilk, the pool liquid is clearer, and Moonmilk is stronger.

Purple and simple ratios

Purple is now 1 Moonmilk : 2 Berry Bliss : 1 Ocean Couch, found in 4 moves. Every simple recipe (all parts 1 to 4) is considered for every colour, so a friendly ratio wins whenever it's close enough.

Pastels and twins

Pastels need lopsided ratios (up to 32 parts white to 1), so the big part may go to 32 while the others stay small. Every target is kept at least Δ 4 from every other.

Twins unlock together

Nine groups of colours are near-copies of each other in the original palette (White, Rolling Paper White and First Exhale; Black and Dave's Not Here; Orange and Up in Smoke; and so on). Each group shares one target and one recipe, and catching it unlocks every twin at once. That leaves 70 colours to chase for 80 terpenes.

Strains and families

Landrace (regular weed) inspires the 16 Minecraft colours. Infusing a seed with one of those terpenes breeds the strain for its hue row, and smoking that strain inspires the row's four tones (pale, bright, dusky, deep). That makes 16 strains × 4 tones = all 64 hemp colours, each reached exactly once. The strains fall into four families, and the Terpinator needs weed of the right family to make that family's terpenes.

Resins grow with families

Landrace gives you 8 resins. Each family's first strain adds the resin its colours need: honey, mud, emerald or grape. Breeding widens what you can mix.

Strain identity

A strain is data on one seed and one bud item, not a block per strain. The planted crop remembers its strain. Each strain gets a name and a place in your strain book.

Inspiration Infuser

  • Infuse: seed + one or two terpenes makes a new strain's seed.

Terpinator

  • The factory: feed it weed, pick any terpene you've unlocked in that weed's family, collect vials.
  • The method dial, three blend ports and codon blending all go away.
  • Landrace weed makes the 16 Minecraft colours.

What the new terpenes make

Hemp and concrete

Each of the 16 Minecraft-colour terpenes gets hemp blocks (with stairs and slabs) and FourTwenty concrete and powder. Vanilla concrete is untouched.

Lamps and trim

FourTwenty's 16 dye-colour lamps, inverted lamps and trims become the Minecraft-colour lamps; their recipes take the terpene instead of dye.

Rugs

Sixteen new rug designs in a Minecraft theme, one per colour.

No vanilla dye anywhere in the terpene system.

Mentor and soundtrack

Mentor

A character in the corner of both machines who notices where you are and nudges you with riddles ("opposites have a way of cancelling out…"). Stay stuck and the hint narrows, but it never hands over a recipe. Toggle it in the machine or the config.

Soundtrack

Seven Crawler Tunes play while a machine is open (toggle on or off): For Mead & Melancholy, La Campagnola, Lunar Midnight, New World Tavern, Pints of Folly, Serenade for Thirteen Flagons, Suite for a Lone Bard. Plus sounds for drops, ripples, bubbles, the map's glow and the match.
